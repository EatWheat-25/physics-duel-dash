1. Kinematics: car speeding up

Chapter 2: Kinematics · easy · 4 marks
Main question · 120s

A car speeds up uniformly from \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) to \(20.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) in \(6.0\,\mathrm{s}\).

Calculate

(a) the acceleration,

(b) the distance travelled.

Steps
Step 1: Initial velocity 1 mark · 15s
What is the initial velocity \(u\)?
  1. \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(20.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(0\)
  4. \(6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
The car starts at \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
Step 2: Final velocity 1 mark · 15s
What is the final velocity \(v\)?
  1. \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(12.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(20.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  4. \(0\)
The car ends at \(20.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
Step 3: (a) Acceleration 1 mark · 15s
What is the acceleration \(a\)?
  1. \(3.3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \(1.3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(12\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
\(a=\dfrac{20.0-8.0}{6.0}=2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).
Step 4: (b) Distance 1 mark · 15s
What is the distance travelled \(s\)?
  1. \(120\,\mathrm{m}\)
  2. \(36\,\mathrm{m}\)
  3. \(84\,\mathrm{m}\)
  4. \(72\,\mathrm{m}\)
\(s=\tfrac{u+v}{2}t=\tfrac{8.0+20.0}{2}\times6.0=84\,\mathrm{m}\).

2. Kinematics: train braking to a stop

Chapter 2: Kinematics · easy · 4 marks
Main question · 150s

A train moving at \(30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) brakes uniformly and stops in a distance of \(450\,\mathrm{m}\).

Calculate

(a) the deceleration,

(b) the time taken to stop.

Steps
Step 1: Initial velocity 1 mark · 15s
What is the initial velocity \(u\)?
  1. \(0\)
  2. \(30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  4. \(450\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
The train starts at \(30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
Step 2: Final velocity 1 mark · 15s
What is the final velocity \(v\)?
  1. \(30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(0\)
  4. \(-30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
The train stops, so \(v=0\).
Step 3: (a) Acceleration 1 mark · 15s
What is the acceleration \(a\)?
  1. \(-1.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \(-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \(-0.067\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(+1.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
\(0=30^{2}+2a(450)\Rightarrow a=-1.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), a deceleration of \(1.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).
Step 4: (b) Time 1 mark · 15s
What is the time taken \(t\)?
  1. \(15\,\mathrm{s}\)
  2. \(30\,\mathrm{s}\)
  3. \(60\,\mathrm{s}\)
  4. \(450\,\mathrm{s}\)
\(t=\dfrac{v-u}{a}=\dfrac{0-30}{-1.0}=30\,\mathrm{s}\).

3. Kinematics: ball dropped from a height

Chapter 2: Kinematics · easy · 4 marks
Main question · 150s

A ball is dropped from rest from a height of \(20.0\,\mathrm{m}\). Air resistance is negligible. Take \(g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

Calculate

(a) the time to reach the ground,

(b) the speed just before it hits the ground.

Steps
Step 1: Initial velocity 1 mark · 15s
What is the initial velocity \(u\)?
  1. \(9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(20.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(0\)
  4. \(1.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
Dropped from rest, so \(u=0\).
Step 2: Acceleration 1 mark · 15s
What is the acceleration \(a\)?
  1. \(0\)
  2. \(9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \(20.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(4.9\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
In free fall \(a=g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) (downwards positive).
Step 3: (a) Time 1 mark · 15s
What is the time taken \(t\)?
  1. \(2.02\,\mathrm{s}\)
  2. \(4.08\,\mathrm{s}\)
  3. \(1.43\,\mathrm{s}\)
  4. \(2.04\,\mathrm{s}\)
\(s=\tfrac12at^{2}\Rightarrow t=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(20.0)}{9.81}}=2.02\,\mathrm{s}\).
Step 4: (b) Speed 1 mark · 15s
What is the final velocity \(v\)?
  1. \(39.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(14.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(19.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  4. \(9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
\(v=\sqrt{2as}=\sqrt{2(9.81)(20.0)}=19.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).

  3. \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)

  1. \(4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)

7. Momentum: two trolleys stick together

Chapter 3: Dynamics · medium · 5 marks
Main question · 180s

A trolley of mass \(2.0\,\mathrm{kg}\) moving at \(3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) hits a stationary trolley of mass \(1.0\,\mathrm{kg}\). They stick together.

Calculate

(a) their speed after the collision,

(b) the kinetic energy lost.

Steps
Step 1: Momentum before 1 mark · 15s
What is the total momentum before, \(p\)?
  1. \(3.0\,\mathrm{N\,s}\)
  2. \(6.0\,\mathrm{N\,s}\)
  3. \(9.0\,\mathrm{N\,s}\)
  4. \(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,s}\)
\(p=2.0\times3.0=6.0\,\mathrm{N\,s}\). The other trolley has none.
Step 2: Total mass 1 mark · 15s
What is the total mass after, \(m\)?
  1. \(2.0\,\mathrm{kg}\)
  2. \(1.0\,\mathrm{kg}\)
  3. \(3.0\,\mathrm{kg}\)
  4. \(6.0\,\mathrm{kg}\)
The trolleys move together: \(2.0+1.0=3.0\,\mathrm{kg}\).
Step 3: (a) Speed after 1 mark · 15s
What is the velocity after, \(v\)?
  1. \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(1.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  4. \(6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
\(v=\dfrac{6.0}{3.0}=2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
Step 4: KE before 1 mark · 15s
What is the kinetic energy before?
  1. \(6.0\,\mathrm{J}\)
  2. \(18\,\mathrm{J}\)
  3. \(3.0\,\mathrm{J}\)
  4. \(9.0\,\mathrm{J}\)
\(\tfrac12(2.0)(3.0)^{2}=9.0\,\mathrm{J}\).
Step 5: KE after 1 mark · 15s
What is the kinetic energy after?
  1. \(6.0\,\mathrm{J}\)
  2. \(12\,\mathrm{J}\)
  3. \(4.0\,\mathrm{J}\)
  4. \(9.0\,\mathrm{J}\)
\(\tfrac12(3.0)(2.0)^{2}=6.0\,\mathrm{J}\), so \(9.0-6.0=3.0\,\mathrm{J}\) is lost.

8. Dynamics: person in an accelerating lift

Chapter 3: Dynamics · medium · 4 marks
Main question · 150s

A person of mass \(60\,\mathrm{kg}\) stands on scales in a lift. The lift accelerates upwards at \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). Take \(g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

Calculate the force the scales exert on the person.

Steps
Step 1: Weight 1 mark · 15s
What is the weight \(W\)?
  1. \(60\,\mathrm{N}\)
  2. \(589\,\mathrm{N}\)
  3. \(120\,\mathrm{N}\)
  4. \(709\,\mathrm{N}\)
\(W=mg=60\times9.81=589\,\mathrm{N}\).
Step 2: Resultant force 1 mark · 15s
What is the resultant force \(F\)?
  1. \(120\,\mathrm{N}\)
  2. \(589\,\mathrm{N}\)
  3. \(30\,\mathrm{N}\)
  4. \(0\)
\(F=ma=60\times2.0=120\,\mathrm{N}\).
Step 3: Direction 1 mark · 15s
Which way is the resultant force?
  1. \(\text{Up}\)
  2. \(\text{Down}\)
  3. \(\text{No direction}\)
  4. \(\text{Sideways}\)
It points the same way as the acceleration: up.
Step 4: Scale force 1 mark · 15s
What is the scale force \(R\)?
  1. \(469\,\mathrm{N}\)
  2. \(589\,\mathrm{N}\)
  3. \(709\,\mathrm{N}\)
  4. \(120\,\mathrm{N}\)
\(R-W=ma\Rightarrow R=589+120=709\,\mathrm{N}\).

10. Power: motor lifting a load

Chapter 5: Work, Energy and Power · easy · 3 marks
Main question · 150s

A motor lifts a load of mass \(80\,\mathrm{kg}\) through a height of \(12\,\mathrm{m}\) at constant speed in \(16\,\mathrm{s}\). Take \(g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

Calculate

(a) the work done on the load,

(b) the power of the motor.

Steps
Step 1: Lifting force 1 mark · 15s
What is the lifting force \(F\)?
  1. \(80\,\mathrm{N}\)
  2. \(785\,\mathrm{N}\)
  3. \(960\,\mathrm{N}\)
  4. \(392\,\mathrm{N}\)
At constant speed the force equals the weight: \(80\times9.81=785\,\mathrm{N}\).
Step 2: (a) Work done 1 mark · 15s
What is the work done \(W\)?
  1. \(9420\,\mathrm{J}\)
  2. \(960\,\mathrm{J}\)
  3. \(589\,\mathrm{J}\)
  4. \(12\,600\,\mathrm{J}\)
\(W=Fs=785\times12=9420\,\mathrm{J}\).
Step 3: (b) Power 1 mark · 15s
What is the power \(P\)?
  1. \(9420\,\mathrm{W}\)
  2. \(150\,\mathrm{W}\)
  3. \(785\,\mathrm{W}\)
  4. \(589\,\mathrm{W}\)
\(P=\dfrac{W}{t}=\dfrac{9420}{16}=589\,\mathrm{W}\).
