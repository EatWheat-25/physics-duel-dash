1. SI base units of pressure (p = F/A)

Physical Quantities · easy · 3 marks · 95s

The pressure \(p\) exerted by a force is given by \[ p=\frac{F}{A} \] where \(F\) is the force and \(A\) is the area it acts on.

Using this equation, determine the SI base units of pressure.

Step 1: Unit of force 1 mark · 15s
What are the SI base units of force \(F\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{N}\)
From \(F=ma\): \(\mathrm{kg}\times\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}=\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}\). The newton is a derived unit, not a base unit.
Step 2: Unit of area 1 mark · 15s
What is the SI base unit of area \(A\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{m}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{m^{-2}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{m^{2}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{m^{3}}\)
Area is length \(\times\) length, so \(\mathrm{m^{2}}\).
Step 3: Unit of pressure 1 mark · 20s
Using \(p=\dfrac{F}{A}\), what are the SI base units of pressure?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{3}\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{Pa}\)
\(\dfrac{\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}}{\mathrm{m^{2}}}=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-2}}\). The pascal is derived, not base.

2. Homogeneity: speed of a wave on a string

Physical Quantities · medium · 4 marks · 120s

The speed \(v\) of a transverse wave on a stretched string is given by \[ v=\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} \] where \(T\) is the tension in the string and \(\mu\) is the mass per unit length of the string.

Use SI base units to determine whether the equation is homogeneous.

Step 1: Unit of tension 1 mark · 15s
Tension is a force. What are the SI base units of \(T\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}\)
Force \(=ma\), so \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}\).
Step 2: Unit of mass per unit length 1 mark · 15s
What are the SI base units of \(\mu\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg\,m}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{kg}\)
Mass per unit length \(=\dfrac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m}}=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}\).
Step 3: Units inside the square root 1 mark · 20s
What are the SI base units of \(\dfrac{T}{\mu}\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{kg^{2}\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-2}}\)
\(\dfrac{\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}}{\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}}=\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-2}}\).
Step 4: Conclusion 1 mark · 20s
Which statement is correct?
  1. Homogeneous: both sides have units m s⁻¹
  2. Not homogeneous: the right-hand side has units m² s⁻²
  3. Not homogeneous: the right-hand side has units m s⁻²
  4. Homogeneous: both sides have units m² s⁻²
\(\sqrt{\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-2}}}=\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), the same as the units of \(v\), so the equation is homogeneous.

3. SI base units of viscosity (F = 6πηrv)

Physical Quantities · medium · 3 marks · 120s

A small sphere of radius \(r\) moving at speed \(v\) through a liquid experiences a drag force \(F\) given by \[ F=6\pi\eta r v \] where \(\eta\) is the viscosity of the liquid.

Determine the SI base units of \(\eta\).

Step 1: Rearrange for η 1 mark · 15s
Rearrange the equation to make \(\eta\) the subject.
  1. \(\eta=\dfrac{6\pi r v}{F}\)
  2. \(\eta=\dfrac{F}{6\pi r v}\)
  3. \(\eta=\dfrac{F r}{6\pi v}\)
  4. \(\eta=\dfrac{F v}{6\pi r}\)
Divide both sides by \(6\pi r v\).
Step 2: Units of rv 1 mark · 15s
What are the SI base units of the product \(rv\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{m\,s}\)
\(\mathrm{m}\times\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}=\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}\). The number \(6\pi\) has no units.
Step 3: Units of η 1 mark · 20s
What are the SI base units of \(\eta\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-1}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{3}\,s^{-3}}\)
\(\dfrac{\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}}{\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}}=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-1}}\).

4. SI base units of capacitance (E = ½CV²)

Physical Quantities · hard · 5 marks · 120s

The energy \(E\) stored in a capacitor is given by \[ E=\tfrac{1}{2}CV^{2} \] where \(C\) is the capacitance and \(V\) is the potential difference across it.

Determine the SI base units of \(C\).

Step 1: Unit of potential difference 1 mark · 20s
Potential difference \(V=\dfrac{P}{I}\). What are the SI base units of \(V\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-3}\,A^{-1}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-3}\,A}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-2}\,A^{-1}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-3}\,A^{-1}}\)
\(\dfrac{\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-3}}}{\mathrm{A}}=\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-3}\,A^{-1}}\).
Sub-step 1: Unit of power 1 mark · 15s
What are the SI base units of power \(P\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-3}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{W}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-3}}\)
Power \(=\dfrac{\text{energy}}{\text{time}}=\dfrac{\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-2}}}{\mathrm{s}}\).
Sub-step 2: Unit of current 1 mark · 15s
What is the SI base unit of current \(I\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{A}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{C}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{A\,s}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{V}\)
The ampere is one of the SI base units.
Step 2: Unit of energy 1 mark · 15s
What are the SI base units of energy \(E\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-3}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-1}}\)
Work \(=\) force \(\times\) distance: \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}\times\mathrm{m}\).
Step 3: Units of capacitance 1 mark · 30s
Using \(C=\dfrac{2E}{V^{2}}\), what are the SI base units of \(C\)?
  1. \(\mathrm{kg^{-1}\,m^{-2}\,s^{4}\,A^{-2}}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-4}\,A^{-2}}\)
  3. \(\mathrm{A\,s}\)
  4. \(\mathrm{kg^{-1}\,m^{-2}\,s^{4}\,A^{2}}\)
\(V^{2}\) has units \(\mathrm{kg^{2}\,m^{4}\,s^{-6}\,A^{-2}}\). Dividing: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-2}}}{\mathrm{kg^{2}\,m^{4}\,s^{-6}\,A^{-2}}}=\mathrm{kg^{-1}\,m^{-2}\,s^{4}\,A^{2}}\). (\(\mathrm{A\,s}\) comes from forgetting to square \(V\).)

9. Uncertainty: g from a pendulum

Uncertainties · hard · 4 marks · 120s

    The acceleration of free fall \(g\) is found using a simple pendulum and the equation

    \[

    g=\frac{4\pi^{2}L}{T^{2}}

    \]

    The measurements are:

  • length \(L=(0.800\pm0.004)\,\mathrm{m}\)
  • period \(T=(1.80\pm0.02)\,\mathrm{s}\)

Determine \(g\) with its absolute uncertainty.

Step 1: Value of g 1 mark · 25s
What is the value of \(g\) calculated from these measurements?
  1. \(9.75\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \(17.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \(2.44\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \(9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
\(g=\dfrac{4\pi^{2}\times0.800}{1.80^{2}}=9.75\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). Use the measurements, not the textbook value.
Step 2: Percentage uncertainty in g 2 mark · 30s
What is the percentage uncertainty in \(g\)?
  1. \(1.6\%\)
  2. \(2.7\%\)
  3. \(0.6\%\)
  4. \(3.8\%\)
\(\%L=0.5\%\), \(\%T=1.1\%\). \(T\) is squared, so \(\%g=0.5+2(1.1)=2.7\%\).
Step 3: Final answer 1 mark · 25s
How should \(g\) be written with its absolute uncertainty?
  1. \((9.75\pm0.27)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  2. \((9.7\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  3. \((9.748\pm0.265)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
  4. \((9.7\pm0.3)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
\(\Delta g=0.027\times9.75=0.26\), given to 1 s.f. as \(0.3\). The value is then rounded to the same decimal place: \(9.7\).
